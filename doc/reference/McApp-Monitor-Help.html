Start here: the five things on your screenshots

Safe to ignore: dropped · command_echo after a reconnect; redirected · blocklist on a response line; 0 dBm / 0 dB on Server lines (and its icon); TEL lines full of zeros; repeated MHeard POS lines (one per station per minute).

FAQ 1. Why so many “shown” lines with a signal report, and what are they?

Short answer: “shown” only means “not filtered”. These lines are the node’s list of stations it hears directly, not new packets.

“shown” is not an event, it is a verdict. It only says “this copy was passed on to the normal views”. Every ordinary copy says it, so an expanded line is full of it. The interesting part is what follows: the time, the link and the signal values.

The lines in your screenshot (DL2JA-1, DB0ED-99, DL2JA-2, DL2UD-1, DF2SI-12 with BLE origin and ×3 to ×6) have no coordinates, no battery and no via path, only a hardware label such as [Heltec V3] and a signal. These are MHeard entries: your node keeps a list of the stations it has heard directly and reports it over Bluetooth, together with the RSSI and SNR of the last packet it received from each. McApp turns each report into a POS-like line so the signal data can be stored and charted.

Why repeated: on the DK5EN fork firmware the node reports a heard station at most once per minute, with the signal of its latest direct packet from that station and an SNR averaged over its recent frames (checked in the fork source, mhPhoneLive). On official firmware the node reports on every update with that one packet’s values. After a Bluetooth reconnect the node also sends its whole MHeard list once, which explains the burst right after “Reconnecting”. Each sub-line is therefore one MHeard report, not necessarily one received packet, and the time shown is when the Pi received the report. The node’s own heard-at time is not shown: it is node-local wall time and only approximate.

Since v2.1.9 these lines are the MH kind, the header summarises them (“14 reports · 13 min · RSSI −118…−117 dBm · SNR −7…−6 dB”), and an expanded line shows the newest five reports with a button for the rest.

Practical use: this is the quickest way to see who your node hears directly and how well. A column of stable values (−126 dBm / −15 dB for DL2JA-1) tells you the link is steady; a station that keeps appearing with SNR near −15 dB is at the edge of what your node can decode.

FAQ 2. Why “TEL” — we have McApp, no telephone?

Short answer: telemetry (sensor readings), not telephone.

TEL means telemetry, not telephone. MeshCom nodes with sensors (or with the sensor fields enabled) periodically broadcast a telemetry frame containing temperature, a second temperature, humidity, air pressure (QFE at station height, QNH at sea level), gas resistance, CO2 and battery level. McApp shows them as TEL and uses them for the WX page.

In your screenshot the values 0°C T2 0°C 0% relH QFE 0 hPa QNH 0 hPa 0 kΩ CO2 0 ppm mean the node has no sensor attached (or none enabled) but still sends the telemetry frame, so the readings are zero. Typically only the battery figure is real, and battery 0% on a powered node generally means “not measured”. DG7RJ-8 on the other hand shows 26.2°C ... 44% relH: a real temperature and humidity sensor.

A TEL line typically appears next to the POS line of the same station and time because nodes usually send them back to back. If they bother you, switch the TEL chip off in the toolbar.

FAQ 3. Why “dropped”?

Short answer: McApp’s own setup command was echoed by the node and hidden on purpose.

The line 18:23:41 MSG RX BLE response → * --ackinfo on dropped · command_echo is McApp talking to your node.

  1. At 18:23:28 the Monitor shows Reconnecting to MC-b878-DK5EN-98: the Bluetooth link was re-established.
  2. On every (re)connect McApp sends the node a few setup commands, among them --ackinfo on, which makes the node report who acknowledged a message.
  3. The node answers each command with a text frame from the fake sender name (pseudo-callsign) response, repeating the command.
  4. That answer is a pure echo of McApp's own housekeeping, so the chat view drops it. The Monitor still lists it so nothing is invisible, and marks it dropped · command_echo.

It is harmless and expected after every Bluetooth reconnect. It only matches a small fixed list of self-sent commands; a reply to a command you typed is not dropped. Other reasons you may see: linkcheck (ping/pong frames) and blocklist (blocked sender).

The yellow bar on the left edge marks lines whose copies were all filtered. Use the Filtered chip to hide them.

FAQ 4. Why different RSSI and SNR values, and where do they come from?

Short answer: your node’s receiver measures them per report and they belong to the last hop; 0/0 means “no radio measurement”.

Source. The values are measured by your node's LoRa receiver at the moment it receives a packet, and are passed to the Pi: over UDP as rssi / snr fields, over Bluetooth in the MHeard report. The backend never calculates them and never rescales them: RSSI is dBm as measured, SNR is already in dB.

Why they differ between lines and copies:

  • Every report is a new measurement. Radio propagation fluctuates constantly (fading, weather, noise), and the MHeard report refers to the latest packet of any type from that station. The same station at −118 dBm / −7 dB and then −8 dB is normal: 1 dB differences are noise, not a change of the link.
  • RSSI and SNR may be computed differently. In an MHeard report the RSSI is that of the latest direct packet, while on the fork firmware the SNR is a running average over recent frames. That is why such SNR values barely move.
  • The measurement belongs to the last hop. For a relayed frame (DF2SI-12 ··· DL2JA-1 →), RSSI/SNR describe the link from DL2JA-1 to your node, not from DF2SI-3 who originated it.
  • Only one copy may carry a value. In the expanded view the UDP copy usually has RSSI/SNR while the BLE copy of the same frame has none (compare DF2KX-1, 18:25:40). The line then shows the one that exists. Copies are not averaged.
  • Placeholder zeros. 0 dBm / 0 dB appears on lines with origin Server (DO2QG-1, DG6YHX-1, OE1XAR-33 {CET}). Those came over the internet from the MeshCom server and were never received by radio, so the node reports 0/0 as “no measurement”. A real reading of exactly 0 dBm / 0 dB is not plausible on LoRa. The full-bars icon next to it is a display artefact (the webapp treats 0 as a reading): ignore it.

How to judge: compare the same station over time in one expanded line. A stable value is a stable link; a trend of falling RSSI and SNR is a link getting worse. Comparing different stations says more about distance and antennas than about link quality.

FAQ 5. Why does --loradebug off say “redirected · blocklist”?

Short answer: harmless. Since v2.1.9 the Monitor says node_reply (“Node reply, moved to group 9999”) instead of “blocklist”, and these lines are the hidden CMD kind.

No real person is blocked here. The sender of these lines is the pseudo-callsign response: that is how McApp labels every reply of your node to a console command (--loradebug off, --txcapture off, --info and so on). The word response is on the curated global blocklist (sperrliste.json), deliberately, so that such replies never appear as chat messages from a station called “response”.

What the verdict means: the destination is * (broadcast). Blocked broadcast and group traffic is not discarded but redirected to the spam group 9999, where you can still inspect it; blocked direct messages would be dropped. The Monitor shows the verdict the chat made, so you see redirected · blocklist instead of dropped.

Why these two lines at 18:30:53 and 18:30:54: you stopped the DBG session. McApp switched on whichever of the debug flags loradebug, txcapture and setlog was off for the session, and now sets back only those to off; the node confirms each with a reply. They are harmless. (The reply to --ackinfo on after a reconnect is hidden by a different rule, dropped · command_echo, because McApp sends that command itself on every connect; the DBG restore replies are not in that short list and take the blocklist path.)

Since v2.1.9 the node's echo of the flag commands McApp itself sends during a DBG session is hidden as command_echo, so you no longer see each of them twice. The same lines appear as DBG rows (--loradebug off at 18:30:58): that is the node's console showing the same command from its own side, not a second reply.

If you want to be sure it is not a leftover rule of your own: the entry comes from the central list, not from a !kb kickban.

Reference

Background for the sections above.

1. The big picture

Your Raspberry Pi runs the McApp backend. It talks to your MeshCom node over two links at the same time:

  • UDP — the node's Extern-UDP feed (JSON, port 1799).
  • BLE — Bluetooth to the same node.

The same radio event often arrives over both links, a fraction of a second apart. The Monitor records every frame the backend receives or sends. It tags each with what happened to it. Identical frames are folded into one line; expand it to see the copies. Nothing here is a second radio: it is the backend's view of the node's traffic.

The Monitor keeps the last 2000 frames in the backend's memory (lost on restart) and up to 5000 lines in the browser. It is a diagnostic view, not a message archive.

2. Anatomy of one line

Part Meaning
18:25:40 Time the backend first saw the frame (your browser's local time).
POS Kind of frame, see section 3.
RX / TX Direction. RX = received. TX = sent by this backend or echoed back by your own node.
LoRa Server BLE Origin: where the frame came from on the mesh. See section 4. A ? means the origin is not known.
DF2KX-1 Sender (source callsign).
DF2KX-12 ··· DL2JA-2 → The relay path (“via”) the frame took, in order. The last station in the list is the one your node actually heard directly. Without a via list there is just an arrow.

payload Text for MSG, coordinates / altitude / distance from you / battery / hardware / firmware for POS, sensor readings for TEL.

BLE UDP Links this frame was seen on. See section 4.
×2 Number of copies folded into this line. Click the line to see each copy.
EEFC312E Message ID (8 hex digits), when the frame has one.

3. Kinds

Badge Meaning
MSG A text message: group, direct or broadcast. Includes {CET} time beacons from the MeshCom server.
POS A position / station beacon. Two flavours: a real position (coordinates, altitude, distance, battery, hardware, firmware) or a signal-only entry without coordinates (see FAQ 1).
MH An entry from your node’s MHeard list (stations it hears directly) with their signal. Not a new packet. Before v2.1.9 these were shown as POS.
TEL Telemetry, not telephone: sensor readings a node broadcasts (temperature, humidity, air pressure QFE/QNH, gas, CO2, battery). See FAQ 2.
ACK An acknowledgement: a text receipt like :ack123, or a delivery-status frame from your node.
SYS Backend notices, mainly Bluetooth state: “Reconnecting to MC-xxxx”, the node's IP address, “finished sending config”.
CMD The node’s reply to a console command (sender response). Hidden by default; enable its chip in the toolbar.
DBG Live lines from the node's debug console. Only exists while you press the DBG button; see section 9.

4. Origin vs. link — two different badges

This is the most common source of confusion. A line carries two independent facts.

Question it answers Where on the line
Origin Where did the frame come from on the mesh? Left, next to RX/TX
Link How did the frame reach the backend on the Pi? Right, after the signal values

Origin badges

LoRa Heard on air by a LoRa radio.
Server Injected from the MeshCom server via an internet gateway, not received by radio. It has no radio signal at all (see FAQ 4, 0 dBm / 0 dB).
BLE Reported by your node over Bluetooth, typically from its MHeard list (see FAQ 1).
Node Your own node's transmission, echoed back.
WWW, ? Web origin / unknown.

Link badges

UDP Arrived over the node's Extern-UDP feed.
BLE Arrived over Bluetooth.
APP Sent by this backend (a message you typed, or an automatic command reply).
CONSOLE Line from the node's debug console.
Example: Origin Server + links UDP and BLE means “the MeshCom server sent it, and your node delivered it to the Pi on both channels”.

5. Merged lines and copies (×N)

The same frame often shows up more than once: UDP and BLE copies of one packet, or a station repeating its beacon. The Monitor folds copies into one line and shows ×N. Click a line (or use Expand all) to see one sub-line per copy:

18:25:40 POS RX BLE DF2KX-1 ... BLE UDP ×2 EEFC312E 18:25:40 BLE shown DF2KX-1 ··· DF2KX-12 ··· DL2JA-2 18:25:40 UDP shown -124 dBm / -13 dB DF2KX-12 ··· DL2JA-2

Each sub-line: time of that copy, the link, the verdict, the signal values of that copy (if it has any), and the via path.

How folding works:

  • MSG, POS and ACK frames with a message ID: same sender and same ID within 60 minutes.
  • MSG without an ID: same sender, destination and text within 2 minutes.
  • POS without an ID: same station at the same position (4 decimals) within 2 minutes.
  • TEL: same station with identical readings within 60 seconds.
  • SYS and DBG lines are never folded. DBG lines have their own cap (500, 2000 or 5000 lines, default 2000, set in the console filter) and do not count against the 5000-row cap of the normal list.

Folding is a display feature: it never changes what is stored or shown elsewhere.

6. RSSI, SNR and the signal icon

Value Meaning Rule of thumb
RSSI (dBm) Received power. More negative = weaker. −100 is good, −120 is near the limit
SNR (dB) How far the signal sits above the noise floor. LoRa can decode below zero. Above 0 good; below about −15 marginal at typical spreading factors

Icon next to the numbers (from SNR): full = better than −4 dB, two bars = better than −8, one bar = better than −12, none = −12 or worse. When a line has several copies, the line shows the most recent copy that carries a measurement.

Two cautions. (1) RSSI and SNR describe the last hop, i.e. the station your node heard directly (the last entry of the via list), not the originator of a relayed frame. (2) 0 dBm / 0 dB is a placeholder meaning “no radio measurement” (see FAQ 4), not a perfect signal.

7. Verdicts: shown, dropped, redirected

Every copy carries a verdict from the same decision function the live chat uses, so what the Monitor says is exactly what the chat did.

Verdict Meaning
shown Delivered to the normal views as usual. Most lines.
dropped · command_echo The node's reply to a command McApp sent by itself on connect (--ackinfo on). Hidden because it is noise (FAQ 3).
dropped · linkcheck A {ping} / {pong} link-check frame. Used internally, never shown as chat.
dropped · blocklist Sender is on the blocklist (curated list plus your own kickbans).
redirected · blocklist Blocked group / broadcast traffic, moved to the spam group instead of the original group. Also applies to node command replies (sender response), see FAQ 5.
sent / failed Outbound frames: handed to the transport, or rejected (the reason follows).
suppressed Your text was a local command (e.g. !wx) and was never transmitted; the reply goes out as its own sent frame.

A line whose copies were all filtered gets a yellow stripe on the left and a chip with the reason. The Filtered toggle hides or shows such lines.

8. Toolbar and status bar

Toolbar (top)

  • Search: callsign (source, destination, via), text or message ID.
  • DBG: starts a live tap on the node's debug console and shows its lines in the same list. ESP32 nodes only; fails with “console busy” if another tool (e.g. meshlogger) holds the node's single console connection. Next to it: Console filter and Pause. All of it is explained in section 9.
  • MSG / POS / MH / TEL / ACK / SYS / CMD: show or hide each kind. The (i) button at the end opens a legend. Highlighted = shown.
  • Filtered: show or hide lines that were dropped or redirected.
  • Expand all: open every line to show its copies. The setting is remembered.
  • N entries and the trash icon: line count, and clear the view (this clears the browser view, not the backend's ring).

Status bar (bottom)

Your own node at a glance: callsign, board and firmware, battery, position, RX n / TX n counters for this session, and the last station heard with its signal values.

The “N new” pill appears when you have scrolled up and newer lines arrived below; click it to jump to the end.

9. DBG: reading the node console

9.1 What DBG is and what it needs

The DBG button in the toolbar opens the node's own debug console (TCP port 2323) and shows every console line as a DBG row among the mesh frames. This is the node's view of itself: what it received (including damaged frames), what it queued, what it sent, and how busy the channel is. Mesh frames tell you what was on the air; the console tells you what the node did with it.

  • ESP32 nodes only. The network console exists on ESP32 boards. An nRF52 board (RAK4631) has no network console, so DBG cannot work there.
  • One client only. The console accepts exactly one connection. While meshlogger is attached, DBG fails with “console busy”; while DBG runs, meshlogger cannot connect.
  • Flags. A session turns on loradebug, txcapture and setlog on the node, each only if it is not on already, and switches back only those it turned on (see 9.7). While a session runs, the toolbar shows a note such as DBG on <board> (fw <version>): loradebug, txcapture, setlog.

9.2 Reading a DBG row

From left to right:

  • Two times. The first is the arrival time at McApp with milliseconds; the second is the node's own clock (HH:MM:SS) when the line carries one. Many lines have no node time at all.
  • Family pill. What kind of console line it is: for example MH-LoRa (the tag of a packet line), LOG STAT, DBG CHANNEL_UTIL, SM, CAP, WDT. Hover over it for a short explanation; many words in the line itself have such a tooltip too.
  • Severity. A coloured stripe on the left plus a word, warn or alarm, so colour is never the only cue. Lines without it are plain information.
  • The line itself. Packet lines are split into chips (see 9.4); everything else is the raw text with the numbers and names highlighted.

9.3 Line families at a glance

9.4 Packet lines and decoding a frame

A packet line (and the LOG RX and LOG ACK lines) is shown as a row of chips instead of one long string. Hover over a chip for its meaning.

  • Type and message id (x followed by 8 hex digits), and hops left: how many more hops the frame was allowed to make when this node received it. On an ACK line the shown number is the whole byte (server flag included); H8n is normal there.
  • S / T / M: server flag (the frame has passed a gateway), track flag, mesh flag (relaying allowed). A letter appears only when the flag is set.
  • Path, originator first, then the relays in order, followed by the destination and the payload.
  • HW (hardware of the originator), FW (firmware version and sub-version) and LH (hardware of the last hop, the station that transmitted this copy), and FCS (the frame checksum as printed).
  • RSSI / SNR with the signal icon (only on LOG RX), and two badges: DUP (an id the node had already seen: a second copy) and OWN (the node's own call is in the path: an echo of its own transmission).
  • raw shows the original text of the line next to the chips.

A CAP row (RX_FRAME / TX_FRAME) shows the raw bytes shortened; its raw button shows the full line and decode opens a table with type, length, message id, the flags byte bit by bit, hops left, path, destination, payload, hardware, modulation, firmware, last-hop hardware, RSSI/SNR and the full hex. The FCS row compares the checksum in the frame with one McApp computes over the received bytes and says OK or FAIL. A failing FCS means the bytes were damaged on the way (or the dump is cut short), so none of the other fields can be trusted.

The same fields now appear on the ordinary RF rows too: each copy of a decoded frame shows its hops left, the S / T / M flags, a warning badge when its FCS check failed, and the hardware of the last transmitting node.

9.5 Reading STAT

Every 300 seconds the node prints one [LOG] STAT line (needs setlog). All counters are for the last five minutes and restart after each line. “Normal” below are values seen on one busy gateway, not limits set by the firmware, except where stated.

Field Meaning Normal / alarming
util Channel occupancy in percent over the 300 s window (receive plus transmit airtime). 6-31 % per window at a busy site. Sustained above 50 % means congestion.
rx, tx Summed airtime in ms. tx should be small against rx. On old ESP32 builds rx is too high (see 9.8).
newid, dup New message ids seen, and duplicate receptions. A mesh produces several copies per frame; about 58 % duplicates were seen.
err CRC and other receive errors. About 5-6 % of receptions at the observed site. Bursts mean collisions or interference.
txn Own transmissions: own, relayed and gateway-fed.
txfail Transmissions aborted by the 15 s transmit watchdog. Must be 0. Above 0 is flagged as an alarm.
ringmax Highest fill of the TX queue, as used/20. 17/20 was seen in a busy hour. At 20/20 frames are dropped.
drop=a/b/c/d/e Frames dropped from the TX queue per priority: 1 ACK and direct message, 2 group and broadcast, 3 relay, 4 position, 5 HEY. A non-zero first or second number means user traffic was lost: alarm. Drops in 3-5 are load shedding: warn.
mh Neighbour count from the neighbour matrix.
heap Free heap in bytes (with setlog on, this is where heap is reported). A falling trend over days is a leak. About 125-129 kB on a Heltec V3 gateway, about 104 kB on a RAK4631.
trk=i/c HEY interval in seconds and consistent HEYs heard since the last reset. The interval doubles from 30 s up to 900 s; a topology change resets it to 30 s. 900/24 on a stable, quiet node.
up Uptime in seconds. A decrease between two STAT lines means the node rebooted.

fw= is the firmware version and build id, t= is the node's millisecond counter. The DM and MBOX lines that follow in the same tick count direct messages (sent, acknowledged, given up) and the store-and-forward mailbox.

9.6 Severity colours and what “noise” hides

McApp rates each console line as plain information, warn (yellow stripe) or alarm (red stripe). The ratings follow the firmware reference.

Rating Lines
alarm [MC-WDT]; RING_DROP_NEW of priority 1 or 2 (user traffic lost); RING_OVERFLOW, RING_REJECT, RETRANSMIT_GIVEUP_DM; a state machine transition with rc other than 0 (except the usual back-off TX_PREPARE -> IDLE rc=-1); RX_RESTART* or TX_DONE with state other than 0 (a radio fault); [LOG] STAT with txfail above 0 or drops in priority 1 or 2; a [BOOT] with reason PANIC, INT_WDT, TASK_WDT or BROWNOUT.
warn RX_OTHER_ERROR, CRC_ERROR, [LOG] ERR; RING_DROP_NEW of priority 3-5 (load shedding), RING_DROP_PRIO, RING_DROP_STALE, RETRANSMIT_GIVEUP; [LOG] RLY q=full; CAPTURE_DROPPED; CHANNEL_UTIL with util of 70 or more; [LOG] STAT with drops in priority 3-5.

ONRXDONE_SLOW is deliberately not flagged: it was background on a gateway that was working normally.

Noise is the high-volume plumbing that is rarely what you are looking for: all state machine (SM) lines, the receive/transmit timer and gate events (RX_TIMEOUT_FIRE, RX_RESTART, RX_RESTARTED, RX_FLAG_PROCESS, TX_GATE_ENTER, TX_START, CAD_FREE, RADIO_TX, RING_PRIO), the bare firmware words receiveflag, transmittedFlag, OnTXDone, OnRxDone, the [CHECK] and MESH : lines, and a [LoRa] timeout. Noise is hidden by default; untick Hide noise in the console filter when you need the full radio trace. A noise line rated warn or alarm (for example RX_RESTART with a non-zero state) is never hidden.

9.7 Console filter, Pause, flags and restore

  • Console filter (next to DBG): Hide noise (on by default); Alarms only (warn / alarm); Mute families, a list of the families present with a line count each (a muted family with no lines stays listed so you can unmute it; Clear mutes undoes all); and Keep, the number of DBG lines held: 500, 2000 or 5000 (default 2000). DBG lines have their own cap and do not count against the 5000-row cap of the normal list. The filter only hides lines from view.
  • Pause: freezes the list so you can read it. Your filters keep applying while paused. The button shows Resume (N new), with the number of lines that arrived meanwhile.
  • Flags. A session turns on loradebug, txcapture and setlog unless already on; a flag whose state the node did not report is left untouched. When you stop it, McApp restores only what it turned on and waits for the node's confirmation. If a confirmation is outstanding, the toolbar says so (“flags may still be on at the node”) and the restore is retried at the next DBG session. The node's replies to the restore commands appear as hidden CMD lines (see FAQ 5).
  • A node that already runs --setlog <CALL> counts as “setlog already on”: McApp leaves it alone, so your callsign filter on the [LOG] RX lines survives the session.
  • Setlog hides two families. While setlog is on, the node does not print [HEAP] and [MC-HWM] lines. This is not a fault: heap is in [LOG] STAT heap=.

9.8 Pitfalls

  • Arrival times are batch times. The console is read in chunks, so several lines share one millisecond. Never compute an interval from the left-hand time. Use the node's own counters in the line: t= (LOG lines), ts= (DBG lines), wait= and lat= (transmit lines).
  • Old ESP32 builds overstate reception time. Builds before firmware commit f2d6532f (2026-08-25) bill every reception as a 255-byte frame. Then CHANNEL_UTIL rx= and STAT rx= (and with them util) are too high, every CRC_ERROR says size=255, and the damaged-bytes dump ends in garbage. A tell-tale: every non-zero rx= is a multiple of 2476 ms. tx= stays correct.
  • The console is best effort. When the connection is congested the node drops bytes, so a line can be missing or cut off. A line that stops in the middle of a field is such a cut.
  • A damaged dump proves nothing. The bytes of a CRC_PAYLOAD dump can be wrong anywhere, including the separators between callsigns. Do not count callsigns from it as senders.
  • Some lines only exist with a flag. CHANNEL_UTIL and ONRXDONE_* need loradebug; with only setlog on they are absent, while STAT util= still works. dedup=100/100 is normal once the ring has wrapped.
  • Direct-message numbering. A direct message carries {NNN with an opening brace and no closing brace, and its acknowledgement is the separate text :ackNNN. A retry flips bits of the message id, so a retry has a different x... id than the original.
  • Line shapes differ by platform. [HEAP] comes with a space or a semicolon after the tag depending on the board and firmware path; McApp accepts both.
